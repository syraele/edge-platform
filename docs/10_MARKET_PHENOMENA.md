# Market Phenomena

Version: 1.0

Status: Foundational

---

## Purpose

This document defines the theoretical model of market phenomena within EDGE_ENGINE.

It is not an implementation document.
It does not describe software components, data structures, interfaces, or runtime behavior.
It defines the conceptual foundation upon which research, knowledge formation, and edge discovery are understood.

This document is intended to remain valid even if the software implementation changes completely in the future.

---

## 1. What is a market phenomenon?

A market phenomenon is a regularity observable in the behavior of a market.

It is a recurring form of structure that appears across observations, and that can be recognized as meaningful by research.

From the perspective of quantitative research, a phenomenon is not merely a random fluctuation, nor a single measurement, nor a single observation.
It is a pattern that has enough stability and repeatability to be considered a subject of investigation.

A market phenomenon exists independently of EDGE_ENGINE.
It is not created by the platform.
The platform does not invent it.
The platform discovers, describes, and organizes it.

The distinction is essential:

- the market contains the phenomenon;
- EDGE_ENGINE studies the phenomenon;
- the platform does not manufacture the phenomenon by its own will.

A phenomenon becomes scientifically relevant when it can be observed, expressed, and validated with evidence.

## 2. Relationship between market, phenomenon, hypothesis, evidence, knowledge, canonical knowledge, candidate edge, and validated edge

The concepts below form a chain of increasing structure and increasing operational meaning.

### Market

The market is the empirical domain of observation.
It is the environment in which prices, volumes, timing, and relationships evolve.
The market is the source of all observable behavior.

### Phenomenon

A phenomenon is a recurring structure or regularity in market behavior.
It is the object of investigation.
It is the conceptual form that connects observations to meaning.

A phenomenon is not yet a conclusion.
It is a pattern that may or may not become scientifically useful.

### Hypothesis

A hypothesis is a structured proposition about a phenomenon.
It expresses a possible relationship between observable conditions and expected outcomes.

A hypothesis is a form of inquiry.
It is not yet proof.
It is a way of turning a phenomenon into a testable question.

### Evidence

Evidence is the observed material that supports or challenges a hypothesis.
It is the empirical basis of the investigation.
Evidence may confirm, weaken, or reject a hypothesis.

Evidence is the bridge between abstract reasoning and observable reality.

### Knowledge

Knowledge is a validated and reusable understanding derived from evidence.

It is the result of a research process that has transformed observations into a meaningful claim.
Knowledge is not simply an opinion, nor a raw observation, nor a single piece of evidence.
It is a structured understanding that can be reused.

Knowledge is therefore a unit of research meaning.

### Canonical Knowledge

Canonical Knowledge is a consolidated representation of a phenomenon or family of related knowledge.

It does not replace Knowledge.
It organizes and stabilizes it.

Canonical Knowledge exists because multiple individual Knowledge items may express the same underlying phenomenon in different forms, with different wording, different evidence contexts, or different levels of detail.
Canonical Knowledge provides a stable, summarized, reusable representation of that shared meaning.

### Candidate Edge

A Candidate Edge is a research result that appears to have potential operational value.

It is not yet fully validated as an operational opportunity.
It is a provisional conclusion that deserves further scrutiny.

A Candidate Edge emerges when a phenomenon, once understood and supported by evidence, is judged to be sufficiently relevant to be considered for future validation.

### Validated Edge

A Validated Edge is a Candidate Edge that has passed the appropriate validation process and is recognized as a robust and reusable research outcome.

It is the point at which a phenomenon becomes an operationally relevant and trustworthy research artifact.

## 3. Does EDGE_ENGINE discover edges or phenomena?

EDGE_ENGINE discovers phenomena first.

It does not discover edges as an initial object of inquiry.
Edges are the later outcome of a longer process of research, validation, and relevance assessment.

The platform is fundamentally a quantitative research engine.
Its first task is to identify, describe, and organize recurring market patterns.
Only after this conceptual understanding has matured can those patterns become candidates for operational relevance.

Therefore:

- the primary object of discovery is the phenomenon;
- the edge is a downstream expression of a validated phenomenon with sufficient relevance.

This distinction is crucial.
If the platform begins by treating edges as the primary object, it risks confusing operational usefulness with scientific understanding.
Scientific understanding must come first.

## 4. Relationship between Knowledge and Phenomenon

A Knowledge item and a Phenomenon are related but not identical.

### A Knowledge describes a phenomenon

A single Knowledge item may describe a phenomenon.
It may express a claim about the regularity, the condition under which it appears, or the meaning of the observation.

However, a Knowledge item is not the phenomenon itself.
It is a representation of it.

### A phenomenon can be observed through many Knowledge items

Yes.
A single phenomenon can be expressed through many Knowledge items.
Different studies, different evidence sets, different descriptions, and different contexts may all point to the same underlying regularity.

This is one of the main reasons why a platform needs a concept beyond isolated Knowledge.

### A single Knowledge can belong to multiple phenomena

Yes, in principle.
A Knowledge item may be broad enough to participate in more than one interpretive structure.

For example, a single observation may contain information relevant to both a reversal pattern and a volatility regime, or may connect to more than one conceptual family.

This means the relation between Knowledge and Phenomenon is not necessarily one-to-one.
It can be one-to-many or many-to-many.

The platform must therefore avoid assuming that every Knowledge item corresponds to only one phenomenon.

## 5. Why does Canonical Knowledge exist?

Canonical Knowledge exists to solve a specific problem: the fragmentation of meaning.

Without it, the system would be forced to treat every Knowledge item as an isolated expression of understanding.
That would create several problems:

- the same phenomenon would appear under multiple formulations;
- the system would lose conceptual compactness;
- related research would remain dispersed instead of being unified;
- future analysis would be less efficient and less stable.

Canonical Knowledge solves this by providing a consolidated and reusable representation of the shared meaning behind multiple Knowledge items.

### Why is Knowledge alone not enough?

Knowledge is necessary, but not sufficient, because it preserves the claim and its evidence, yet it does not by itself guarantee that the system can recognize the shared pattern behind many different claims.

Knowledge is a unit of validated conclusion.
Canonical Knowledge is a unit of consolidated understanding.

The former preserves the specificity of a claim.
The latter preserves the continuity of a phenomenon across many claims.

This is why Canonical Knowledge is needed.

## 6. When does a phenomenon become an Edge?

A phenomenon becomes an Edge when it crosses the threshold from being a pattern of interest to being a usable research opportunity.

The logical passage is as follows:

1. A phenomenon is observed.
2. A hypothesis is formulated about it.
3. Evidence is collected.
4. Knowledge is formed.
5. The phenomenon is recognized as recurrent and meaningful.
6. Its relevance is assessed in a broader research context.
7. It is judged to have sufficient operational value to be treated as a Candidate Edge.
8. After validation, it becomes a Validated Edge.

The transformation from phenomenon to edge is therefore not a change in the underlying market reality.
It is a change in the status of the research conclusion.

A phenomenon may be real and scientifically meaningful without being operationally valuable.
An Edge is the moment when a phenomenon becomes relevant enough to be considered as an actionable research outcome.

## 7. The true objective of EDGE_ENGINE

The true objective of EDGE_ENGINE is not to produce trading actions.
It is not to optimize execution.
It is not to create strategies in the narrow sense.

Its mission is to function as a quantitative research engine.

Its purpose is to:

- observe market behavior;
- formulate rigorous questions;
- generate evidence;
- transform evidence into validated knowledge;
- recognize recurring phenomena;
- consolidate meaning across multiple observations;
- preserve research understanding in a reusable form;
- support the emergence of robust and validated edges.

In this sense, EDGE_ENGINE is a platform for the scientific discovery of market structure.

Its deepest mission is not to decide what to do in the market.
Its deepest mission is to increase the quality, coherence, and reusability of quantitative understanding.

That is the true foundation of the platform.
