# KF-004.1 — Phenomenon Model

Version: 0.1

Status: Proposed

---

## Objective

Define the conceptual model of a market phenomenon in EDGE_ENGINE before introducing any recognition algorithm.

The purpose is to clarify what a phenomenon is, how it relates to Knowledge, and how it can later be compared and consolidated without changing the existing Domain Model.

---

## 1. What is a market phenomenon in EDGE_ENGINE?

A market phenomenon is an observable, repeatable, and semantically meaningful pattern in market behaviour.

It is not merely a single sentence or a single measurement.
It is the underlying regularity that multiple observations may point to.

In EDGE_ENGINE, a phenomenon is the stable object of interpretation that emerges from evidence and becomes recognizable across multiple Knowledge items.

A phenomenon is therefore:

- observable in market data;
- grounded in evidence;
- expressed through one or more Knowledge items;
- potentially reusable as a conceptual unit for future analysis.

---

## 2. Does a phenomenon arise from a single Knowledge or from a set of related Knowledge?

A phenomenon does not originate from one isolated Knowledge item.

It emerges from a set of related Knowledge items that describe the same underlying market regularity from different perspectives, evidence contexts, or formulations.

A single Knowledge item may be the first observation of a phenomenon, but the phenomenon becomes conceptually meaningful only when several related Knowledge items converge around the same structure.

In this sense:

- a Knowledge item is a claim or interpretation grounded in evidence;
- a phenomenon is the shared pattern that those claims refer to.

A phenomenon is therefore a higher-level abstraction than a single Knowledge item.

---

## 3. Difference between Knowledge, Phenomenon, and Canonical Knowledge

### Knowledge

Knowledge is a validated, reusable research conclusion.

It is the unit of meaning that results from evidence and validation.
It preserves the traceability to the original evidence and remains attached to a specific claim.

### Phenomenon

A Phenomenon is the recurring market pattern that one or more Knowledge items refer to.

It is not a claim. It is a conceptual object describing what the market is doing in a recognizable and reusable way.

It exists at a level above individual Knowledge items and below the full research process.

### Canonical Knowledge

Canonical Knowledge is a distilled representation of a phenomenon for reuse.

It is not the phenomenon itself.
It is the system’s preferred, consolidated expression of the phenomenon for future use.

Canonical Knowledge may be derived from one or more Knowledge items that describe the same phenomenon, but it remains a representation of that phenomenon rather than the phenomenon itself.

In short:

- Knowledge = a validated claim;
- Phenomenon = the underlying observable pattern;
- Canonical Knowledge = a consolidated representation of that pattern.

---

## 4. Lifecycle of a phenomenon inside the system

A phenomenon should be understood as an evolving concept rather than a static label.

### Stage 1 — Observation

A phenomenon begins when one or more Knowledge items point to a recurring structure in market behaviour.

### Stage 2 — Recognition

As more Knowledge items are generated, the system can recognize that they refer to the same underlying pattern.

### Stage 3 — Consolidation

The system may group these Knowledge items under a shared phenomenon identity and create a consolidated representation.

### Stage 4 — Reuse

Once recognized, the phenomenon can be reused as a conceptual unit for candidate-edge qualification, validation, or future research.

### Stage 5 — Evolution

A phenomenon may evolve as more evidence is collected. Its description may become more precise, broader, or refined over time.

This lifecycle is conceptual and does not require a new domain entity in the current model. It can be represented as an application-level projection or registry.

---

## 5. What belongs to the phenomenon and what remains part of a single Knowledge?

This distinction is essential.

### Information that belongs to the phenomenon

The following belongs to the phenomenon as an abstraction:

- the underlying market regularity;
- the observable pattern family;
- the trigger structure;
- the directional and temporal character of the effect;
- the market context in which it appears;
- the consolidated interpretation shared across multiple Knowledge items.

These are properties of the phenomenon itself, not of a single claim.

### Information that remains specific to a single Knowledge

The following remains specific to the individual Knowledge item:

- the original statement;
- the exact evidence reference;
- the provenance of the specific validation event;
- the original metadata attached to that Knowledge item;
- the lifecycle state of that Knowledge item.

These properties describe the claim, its evidence, and its validation history, not the phenomenon as such.

---

## Conceptual boundary

The Phenomenon Model should therefore be designed as a representation of a shared market regularity, not as a metadata wrapper around a single Knowledge item.

Its purpose is to capture what multiple Knowledge items are collectively pointing to.

---

## Design direction

At this stage, the system should treat a Phenomenon as:

- an observable market pattern;
- a shared abstraction over related Knowledge items;
- a conceptual unit that can later support equivalence, similarity, and consolidation.

The formal invariants, equivalence rules, similarity logic, and clustering strategy will be defined only after this conceptual boundary is approved.

### Core semantic attributes

- phenomenon_type
  - the high-level family of the effect, for example: reversal, continuation, breakout, volatility expansion, gap, mean reversion.
- direction
  - whether the effect is bullish, bearish, or neutral with respect to the expected movement.
- trigger_condition
  - the observable condition that makes the phenomenon manifest, for example: close above open, body larger than range, previous close break.
- temporal_scope
  - the horizon over which the effect is expected to appear, for example: immediate, short-term, medium-term.
- magnitude
  - the strength or intensity of the effect, expressed as a quantitative descriptor rather than a hard-coded threshold.

### Contextual attributes

- market_context
  - instrument, timeframe, and market regime.
- regime_state
  - trend, range, high-volatility, low-volatility, session-specific state.
- observation_window
  - the historical window from which the observation was derived.

### Evidence-oriented attributes

- evidence_signature
  - the measurable pattern that makes the phenomenon observable.
- robustness
  - how stable or repeatable the effect appears across evidence.
- provenance
  - the original Knowledge or Evidence source that produced the descriptor.

---

## 2. Attributes derived from existing Knowledge

The Phenomenon Model should derive its initial content from information already present in the current Knowledge model.

### Directly derivable from existing Knowledge

- statement
  - the natural-language description of the observed effect.
- metadata
  - measurements and descriptors already attached to Knowledge, such as measurement count, threshold values, and indicator names.
- evidence_reference
  - the link to the evidence basis that supports the Knowledge item.
- knowledge_id
  - the stable identifier of the original Knowledge item.

### Derived interpretation

From these existing fields, the system can infer:

- the phenomenon family;
- the directional polarity of the effect;
- the trigger condition expressed by the evidence;
- the strength descriptor implied by the measurements;
- the evidence-backed context in which the effect was observed.

This means the Phenomenon Model is a structured interpretation of existing Knowledge, not a second source of truth.

---

## 3. Attributes needed in the future for Similarity

These attributes are not required for the first milestone, but they are the ones that will later support similarity scoring.

### Similarity-ready attributes

- pattern_signature
  - canonical symbolic representation of the trigger condition.
- direction_vector
  - positive/negative/neutral orientation.
- structural_features
  - body size, range size, breakout distance, momentum differential, and other quantitative descriptors.
- temporal_alignment
  - the horizon and lag associated with the effect.
- context_profile
  - market regime, asset, timeframe, and session conditions.
- robustness_score
  - how stable the effect is across samples.
- evidence_consistency
  - how coherent the supporting evidence is across multiple observations.

These features are intended for future similarity computation, but they should be introduced as derived descriptors rather than as mandatory domain-model changes.

---

## 4. Representation without modifying the Domain Model

The Domain Model remains unchanged.

### Recommended representation

Introduce a new application-level concept:

- PhenomenonModel

This model is not an Aggregate Root and does not replace Knowledge.
It plays the role of a projection or descriptor of one or more Knowledge items.

### Representation shape

A PhenomenonModel should contain:

- knowledge_id
- phenomenon_type
- direction
- trigger_condition
- temporal_scope
- magnitude
- market_context
- regime_state
- evidence_signature
- robustness
- similarity_features

### Storage strategy

To avoid changes to the Domain Model, the representation can be stored in one of these ways:

1. as a sidecar artifact keyed by knowledge_id;
2. as a structured payload inside Knowledge.metadata;
3. as an application-layer registry that maps knowledge_id to PhenomenonModel.

The preferred approach is the application-layer registry or sidecar artifact, because it keeps the Domain Model stable and does not overload the semantics of Knowledge.

---

## Proposed principle

Two Knowledge items should be considered to describe the same phenomenon when they share:

- the same phenomenon family;
- the same trigger logic;
- compatible direction;
- compatible market context;
- compatible temporal scope.

This is a semantic equivalence rule, not a strict equality rule.

---

## Design decision

The Phenomenon Model should be treated as:

- a derived descriptor of Knowledge;
- a bridge between raw Knowledge and future similarity computation;
- an application-level concept that preserves the existing Domain Model intact.
